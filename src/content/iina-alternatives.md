# IINA alternatives for Mac

IINA is a free, open-source video player for the Mac, built on mpv. If it doesn't suit you, these are the main alternatives: VLC, Infuse, Elmedia Player, Movist Pro, mpv and Leah. This page is written by the maker of Leah. Every other player's details come from its developer's own site or App Store listing.

## VLC

VLC, from the non-profit VideoLAN, is free and open source, and runs on Macs with Apple silicon or Intel processors back to Mac OS X 10.7.5. It plays nearly every file format, network streams and discs, and can send video to a Chromecast. It has many settings, filters and tools. See [Leah vs VLC](leah-vs-vlc).

## Infuse

Infuse, by Firecore, builds a library of your movies and shows with artwork from TMDB, from your Mac, network shares, cloud storage and media servers such as Plex, Emby and Jellyfin. It runs on Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple TV and Vision Pro, and syncs your library across them. It's free to start; Infuse Pro, at $1.99 a month, $16.99 a year or $99.99 once, adds lossless audio, cloud services, AirPlay and more. See [Leah vs Infuse](leah-vs-infuse).

## Elmedia Player

Elmedia Player, by Eltima, runs on Macs with Apple silicon or Intel processors with macOS 10.13 or later. The free version plays files and playlists and searches for subtitles online. PRO, from $19.95, adds streaming to Apple TV, Chromecast and DLNA devices, Picture in Picture, an equalizer and A-B loops. On the Mac App Store it comes as two apps: a free one with a PRO upgrade, and a $19.99 one. See [Leah vs Elmedia Player](leah-vs-elmedia).

## Movist Pro

Movist Pro costs $17.99 from its own site, where one license covers up to three Macs in a household, and it's also in Setapp. It needs macOS 12 or later, plays MKV with Dolby TrueHD and PGS and VobSub subtitles, and has Picture in Picture, AirPlay and browser extensions. The Mac App Store has a lighter app, Movist, for $9.99.

## mpv

mpv is the free, open-source player IINA is built on. It has a minimal interface: you control it mostly with the keyboard, configuration files and Lua or JavaScript scripts. It plays the formats FFmpeg supports, including Dolby TrueHD and PGS subtitles. Builds come from mpv's GitHub page or Homebrew, not the Mac App Store.

## Leah

Leah is a video player for Macs with Apple silicon and macOS 15 or later, built from the ground up for fast seeking: in Leah's own tests, a 5-second skip shows the new picture in about 30 milliseconds. It plays MKV and WebM with lossless Dolby TrueHD in up to 7.1 channels, DTS, and PGS and VobSub subtitles, and deinterlaces DVD and TV recordings as they play. It has few settings, collects no data and never connects to the internet. It costs $9.99, once, on the [Mac App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6816698102).

Leah doesn't play VP8, Xvid or Vorbis, and has no Picture in Picture, playlists, plugins or network streams. If you need those, VLC, IINA or Elmedia Player suit you better. See [Leah vs IINA](leah-vs-iina) and [Fast seeking](fast-seeking-video-player-mac).

## Which one to choose

- **Free and plays everything:** VLC.
- **A library on your TV and phone too:** Infuse.
- **Streaming to a TV, on any Mac:** Elmedia Player.
- **Scripts and config files:** mpv.
- **Fast seeking, Blu-ray rips and privacy, for one price:** Leah.
